The journey

Opening

My journey to Aotearoa began with a Level 7 Diploma in Supply Chain and Logistics at Ara Institute of Canterbury, a qualification that gave me a deep appreciation for the systems that keep the world moving: planning, coordination, and accountability. Little did I know that these same principles would shape how I approach every role I’ve held since.

Chapters

The path

  1. Hospitality

    Distinction Hotel

    My first step into the workforce was as a Kitchen Assistant at Distinction Hotel Christchurch. Far from glamorous, it was foundational. I approached every task with the same commitment — no job too small, every detail worth getting right. That attitude was noticed. Management offered me a move to the Front Office, where I discovered a genuine passion for connecting with people, resolving challenges, and making others feel welcome.

  2. Frontline

    COVID-19 response

    When COVID-19 struck and the hotel became a government-managed isolation facility, I stayed on the frontlines. Supporting people isolated far from their families, I understood that care is not just a profession — it is a calling. The New Zealand Government’s COVID-19 Response Recognition Award was a humbling affirmation of that purpose.

  3. Aged care

    Summerset & Chatswood

    From hospitality, I moved into aged care, taking on a Caregiver role at Summerset Retirement Village and later becoming a Caregiver Coordinator at Chatswood Retirement Village. Overseeing care delivery, managing schedules, and supporting staff deepened my leadership and reinforced my commitment to dignity and compassion in service.

  4. Support work

    Tāngata whai ora

    As a Support Worker, I had the privilege of supporting tāngata whai ora — people living with intellectual disabilities and mental health needs. I assisted with daily living, managed medications, and drew on my multilingual abilities in Malayalam, Hindi, Tamil, and English to build trust across cultures. Every interaction reminded me that to be truly helpful, you must first make someone feel seen.

  5. Today

    Ministry of Social Development

    Today, as a Case Manager at the Ministry of Social Development in Kaikōura, these threads have come together. The logistics mindset, the hospitality instinct, and the care-sector heart — all of it informs how I show up for the people I serve every day.

  6. Advocacy

    Health and Safety Representative

    Alongside my role as a Case Manager, I am also the Health and Safety Representative (HSR) for the Kaikōura Work and Income office, representing workers on health and safety matters, identifying and raising workplace risks, supporting consultation, and promoting a safe and healthy working environment. This role has further strengthened my leadership, advocacy, communication, and commitment to creating a positive workplace for others.
